## English

**App name** (30 max): `CyberScan: Check Files & Links`

**Short description** (80 max):
`Check files, links and messages for scams and malware, right on your phone.`

**Full description:**

```
Got a strange file, a link in a text message, or a "your parcel is on hold" SMS? Check it with CyberScan before you open it.

CyberScan takes files, links and messages apart without running them and explains what it finds in plain words. Every check happens on your phone: no account, no uploads, no ads, no tracking.

WHAT IT CHECKS
• Files: documents, PDFs, Office files, archives, pictures, Android apps (APK) and Windows programs. It spots double extensions (invoice.pdf.exe), hidden characters that disguise a file's real type, macros, embedded scripts, packed or encrypted parts, and data hidden after the end of a picture.
• Links: look-alike sites pretending to be your bank, PayPal, Apple, Microsoft, Absher, delivery companies and more; letters from other alphabets (paypal written with a Cyrillic "a"); shortened links; bare IP addresses; risky domain endings; login and payment bait words; links that download programs.
• Messages: SMS, e-mail or chat scams: urgency, requests for codes and passwords, fake fees and prizes, and the links inside.
• QR codes: read the code with the camera, then check the link inside before opening it.

NEW IN 1.2
• Share to CyberScan: in any app, tap Share and choose CyberScan to check a file, link or message instantly. Several files at once are checked together.
• Home-screen widget: your latest result and a Quick scan button.
• New files: choose a folder once and CyberScan lists the files that arrived since you last opened it, ready to check.

TOOLS
• Verify a download: compare a file's fingerprint (SHA-256, SHA-1, MD5) with the one the publisher shows.
• Password strength: how long a fast offline attack would need, checked on the phone and never sent.

CLEAR RESULTS
Each check gets a score from 0 to 100 and one of three verdicts: no warning signs, needs a closer look, or suspicious. Every finding says what was seen and why it matters. The score is an explainable indicator, not a probability, and CyberScan says so: no static check can prove a file is safe.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• Everything is checked on this device; the release app does not even have internet permission.
• The camera is used only to read QR codes, and nothing it sees is saved.
• Your history stays on your phone until you clear it.
• Available in English and Arabic.

CyberScan is a defensive tool that helps you decide. It doesn't replace antivirus software or your own judgement.
```

**What's new (1.2.0):**
```
• Share to CyberScan: check files, links and messages straight from any app
• Home-screen widget with your latest result and a Quick scan button
• New files: see files added to a folder you choose since last time
• Finding cards now draw correctly
```

## Data safety form (answers)

Based on what the 1.2.0 release build does (`phone/lib`, `android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`,
`android/app/src/release/AndroidManifest.xml`, which removes INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE).

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **No** |
| Is all of the user data collected by your app encrypted in transit? | Not applicable (nothing is transmitted). If the form insists: Yes. |
| Do you provide a way for users to request that their data is deleted? | Not applicable: no data leaves the device. History is deleted in the app (Settings > Clear history) or by uninstalling. |
| Independent security review | No |

Why "No" is accurate:
- Files, links and messages are analysed in the app's process; nothing is uploaded. The release
  build has no INTERNET permission, so it cannot send data.
- Shared files are copied to the app's private cache only to read them and are deleted after the
  check. The watched folder is read through the Storage Access Framework, only after the user picks
  it, and only file names/dates and the files the user chooses to scan are read.
- History and settings are stored locally (shared preferences). "Data processed only on the device
  is not collected" per Google's definition.
- Camera frames (QR) are processed on the device by the bundled ML Kit model and never stored.
- "Share report" uses the Android share sheet: the user chooses where it goes, which Play does not
  count as collection by the app.

Re-check this if a future version adds any network feature (cloud lookups, crash reporting,
analytics) or removes `android/app/src/release/AndroidManifest.xml`.

Permissions declaration: CAMERA (QR codes; runtime prompt). No storage, location, contacts, SMS or
notification permissions are requested. No `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`, no accessibility service.
